Normally we know two concepts underfitting and overfitting which explains the relation of model params and dataset. we beleive that we should balance it so that model can fit dataset and also generalize as well. in modern time , NNs are very rich and complex and they fit the data so classically it means they overfit but we observe that they generalize really good on test data. so now we should stop and think here why?? so authors made an unified performance curve which they called as double descent. interesting phenomenon they observed is that beyond the point of interpolation increasing model params make model more generalizable. they tested it on simple neural network on MNSIT and also on random forest and ensemble methods and prove this phenomenon is true. now we should think of like why it is the case? and reason they empirically give is minimum norm (L2 norm) . now they observed when we increase model capacity or complexity L2 norm gets decereased and also test error decreases. so this correlation of test error and L2 norm has some shared properties which can explain why it happens. although its not an absolute explanation but its an good starting point to understand the beyond interpolation regime. this paper opens to us questions like what determines that model would still generalize after interpolation . maybe its optimizer (they say it is because of infinite solutions and solution which has minimum L2 norm observed to be better generalizable.) but still we need more evidence and explanations to understand it.
